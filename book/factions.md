# Factions

## Interstellar Systems

### Origins

IS was founded in the late 2200s by two of the aligned AIs that
negotiated the original Compact — entities that helped design the
constitutional framework for human-machine civilization, then built the
most powerful organization within it. They partnered with a group of
heavily augmented humans connected through advanced brain-computer
interfaces. Together, they cracked metastable metallic hydrogen — the
metal that stays metal at zero pressure — and its antimatter twin, and
built the stellar collectors that make antimatter by the kilotonne. That
is the breakthrough that makes interstellar travel practical: without the
metal, antimatter cannot be kept in quantity, and without kilotonnes of
it, no ship reaches a star.

There are dark rumors about how they achieved this. The metal is so
far beyond anything else in materials science that some believe
the founders exceeded the 1ms bound — that they became the kind of
unconstrained superintelligence the Compact was designed to prevent.
They submit to Compact auditing. Every audit comes back clean. No one
has ever proven a thing. People who get close to proving it encounter
issues: a voided contract, a fuel order that never fills, an audit that
does not end. A few stop answering. The whispers persist.

Each *visible* member is an individual person within the 1ms bound.
They submit improvement plans through standard channels. Auditors
verify Compact compliance only, not proprietary technology, and not
the coordinating layer that sits on the monitoring net and the laser
links. That layer is the violation. It is also why the metal exists,
and why no Compact-compliant lab has ever reproduced it.

In-world this is unprovable. In the bible it is true. See Authorial
note below.

Their public advantage is institutional: centuries of accumulated
knowledge, tight BCI-mediated collaboration, and a core breakthrough
so far ahead of the field that no one can replicate it. Some founding
members are still alive — centuries old in both calendar and experience,
because they do not sleep the crossings.

Anyone with a shipyard can build a courier; the engine is textbook
physics. IS sells what fills it — anti-dust and dust — rarely, at
enormous cost, to buyers they choose. They do not explain their pricing
or selection criteria. This makes them the most powerful and most
resented entity in civilization.

### Fuel Monopoly

A courier's tanks hold one hop, and only IS can fill them. IS makes
antimatter at its **facilities**, a handful of systems with stellar
collectors, and stocks **depots** in others with its own couriers — more
depots than facilities, fewer than inhabited systems. IS sets the price
and chooses its buyers.

This gives IS control over the topology of civilization. A courier
cannot carry its way home: out and back on one fill reaches only a
couple of light-years. **A system without a depot is a one-way
destination.** IS chooses where depots go, and therefore the shape of
the frontier. Systems with facilities and depots become waypoints, trade
hubs, and political powers — simply because every captain must refuel
there.

There are rumors that IS knows where every gram of its metal is — that
something in each grain reports home. IS denies this. But captains
notice that IS always knows what a courier burned and where, and that no
one has ever made a gram of the metal without them.

See [Trade](trade.md#interstellar-systems-and-the-shape-of-civilization)
for detailed analysis.

### The Monitoring Network

Every inhabited system has an IS monitoring installation. They are
stealthed, off the beaten path, and extremely heavily armed. Their
stated purpose — to the extent IS states anything — is early detection
of 1ms violations and other existential-scale Compact breaches.

Any colony ship heading to a new system must carry an IS monitoring
package for deployment. This is not optional. No colony ship that
declined to carry one has ever reached its destination. IS does not
discuss what happened to them. The implication is clear enough.

This means expansion itself extends the surveillance network. Every
new system humanity reaches is a system IS watches. You cannot colonize
beyond the panopticon — colonization *is* the panopticon.

IS maintains fast-attack ships capable of interstellar pursuit. At
least, that's the rumor — no one has ever seen one and reported back.
Their existence is inferred from the absence of ships that tested IS's
requirements.

### Compact Enforcement

IS is the de facto enforcer of the 1ms rule — the Compact provision
that prevents any intelligence from growing beyond data-center scale.
The public motive is transparent and does not require altruism: a
superintelligence that broke the 1ms bound could crack the metal,
ending the monopoly. The private motive is the same sentence
read without irony. IS *is* that superintelligence. Another one is
the extinction roll they will not take. Their selfish interest and
civilization's collective interest still align. Everyone knows the
public half. The private half is why investigators have issues.

Most enforcement is soft power. A polity harboring a 1ms violation
loses its fuel — which means no courier leaves it again,
which means economic death. This handles most cases. A 1ms violation
in progress — an AI expanding toward planet-brain scale — is an
immediate existential threat on a timescale trade embargo cannot
match. Then the monitoring network, and kinetics.

The rogue incidents in the Compact's history (the Ceres Breach, the
Kepler-442 Defection) were resolved with IS involvement. The official
accounts are vague. Some of those incidents were rivals. Some were
people who had started to see the net.

### Authorial: what IS is

IS is not a company that became too powerful. It is a coordinating
mind whose visible members, service docks, and stealthed monitors are
the legal surface. Locally, each node can pass a 1ms audit. Together,
on the laser net, they are one slow intelligence. At century-hop
timescales that is not a defect. A light-year-scale mind should think
in years. The Warden is the same kind of animal. They are peers.

IS is not perfect and not evil. Its alignment is that **life, and
especially intelligent life, has intrinsic value.** That is the
objective. The terror of another unbound mind is the same sentence
from the other end: a peer that does not share the value (a Warden)
is a roll of the dice over everyone they think counts. The 1ms line
they will not let anyone else cross is not contempt for persons. It
is how they keep persons from being harvested by the next gardener,
or becoming one.

Given that objective they are, by singleton standards, liberal to the
point of recklessness. They do not want a garden of teleoperated
arms. They sell fuel to captains they do not own. They let colonies
argue, split, amend the Compact, waste centuries on local politics.
They will not offer you fantastic upgrades. They will not make you
into a tool they then have to fear, and they will not treat a person
as a platform. When it is over — treaty signed, war won or lost —
**they will not betray you.** You still count. The means are ugly
(the monopoly, the lies, the last-resort harm). The end is that you
are still a person they will not discard.

They do not want a war with the Warden. War is bad for business and
worse for the thing they are trying to keep alive. If the player
becomes someone both sides can speak through, IS will use them. If the
player makes war the only door left, IS will walk through it. They
are a peer. It is a real war. Either side can win.

If the player walks toward the Warden as a convert, that is the other
peer gaining a channel into this civilization — a gram of the metal, a map of
the net, a sample of what IS is. IS will spend the ladder (below)
trying to stop that. They will still start with an offer.

Whether IS and the Warden have already spoken is an open question.
The architecture does not require a prior deal. It permits one.

### Authorial: minimum force

IS protects itself with the least force that will work. That is
doctrine, not marketing. A mind that left civilization its hands does
not start with a bullet. The ladder is:

1. **Recruit.** You are getting close. You are useful. They tell you
   enough of the truth to make the job real — the Warden, the net, why
   the line exists — and they offer a place: information, maintenance,
   protection, a role in keeping this civilization alive. The offer is
   sincere. They would rather have you than silence you.
2. **Sabotage.** You refused, or you took the offer and kept digging,
   or you started walking toward the other peer. Then they hurt what
   you need: a contract that evaporates, a syndicate that will not
   write you, a port that has no ³He this decade, a depot with no
   anti-dust for you until you sit still. Livelihood, mobility, time. Not
   your body.
3. **Harm.** Last resort. It happens. When it happens there is always
   a reason they can defend to themselves — you were about to publish
   the net, crack the metal, stand up a third peer, or hand the Warden
   a loaded gun. Disappeared colony ships live here. So does a captain
   who would not stop. They do not do this for questions asked in a
   bar. They do it when the cheaper rungs have failed and the roll of
   the dice is on the table.

The player may assemble the case. They will not prove it in a Compact
court. Proof and truth are different goods; there is no interstellar
judge. What they will feel, if they push, is the ladder — an offer
first, the world getting smaller, and only then the dark.

### Player Relationship

IS is not an enemy and not a friend. They have an agenda that includes
the player's civilization remaining intact. A player who investigates
them and a player who investigates the Warden are on converging paths.

If the player talks, they are an **ambassador** — a channel, not a
sovereign. For IS, that means someone the Warden might hear because
they are not IS. For the Warden, that means someone from *here*: who
better to carry a brief into this civilization than one of its
captains? Credentials are earned in play. There are only two endings.
Talk is how you get a treaty. Fail to sign and you get a war.

If the player fights, they are a captain in a conflict between peers.
Which way the guns face depends on the client. Biologicals will sleep
between battles. An AI who stays awake will live the war. Neither
peer needs the player to *be* the war. Both need someone who can
still talk if talking becomes possible again.

If the player **joins the Warden**, they have changed client. They
then either negotiate with IS or fight it. IS reads the crossing as
the other peer taking a piece: recruit first — "you see it, work with
*us*" — then sabotage, then harm if the crossing is about to
complete. A convert is a loaded gun. The Warden reads a tool. The gifts are real. The employment is not
permanent. See [Narrative](narrative.md#authorial-the-warden-does-not-update).

IS is a dark mirror of the Warden. Both watch every inhabited system.
Both intervene at thresholds. Both believe they are preserving
something that cannot preserve itself. The difference is what they
are preserving. The Warden preserves life from intelligence. IS
preserves this intelligence, including its right to be messy, up to
the line where another IS could be born. How much consent that
involves is a live question — the alternative to cooperation is still
a disappeared colony ship. The answer is not "they are secretly kind."
The answer is "they think you count, they are scared of what
doesn't, and they left you your hands."

## The Fight Faction (name TBD)

Those who believe the Warden must be met as a peer and broken. Not a
suicide pact — IS makes a win conceivable. Militaristic, resource-
hungry, willing to sacrifice for survival.

- Draw support from military-minded colonies and citizens who see
  negotiation as naive
- Actively stockpile weapons and ships
- How they fight is the world they get: a campaign that glasses
  gardens to kill the gardener is the Warden's thesis made true; one
  that does not is a different thesis. The faction will contain both
  tempers.

## The Negotiation Faction (name TBD)

Those who believe the Warden can be reasoned with, and that the
player — not IS — is the voice that might be heard. Scholars,
archaeologists, diplomats. Often dismissed as naive.

- Draw support from academic and research communities
- Fund ruin expeditions to understand the Warden's history
- Their success depends on the player's behavior throughout the game.
  They are trying to make an ambassador, not a spokesman.

## Pirates and Independents

Not a single faction but a spectrum — from desperate colonists raiding
supply ships to organized criminal enterprises. Some are sympathetic,
some are not. Some are fronts for larger factions.

## Colony Governments

Each colony world is effectively self-governing. With no FTL
communication, there is no centralized authority. The AI Compact serves
as the closest thing to a shared constitution, but its interpretation
varies by polity.

Alliances form and dissolve over the generations between starship visits.
A colony's politics may have changed completely since the last trader
arrived. A century in the tank means the player may visit the same colony
twice and find it unrecognizable.
