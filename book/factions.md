# Factions

## Interstellar Systems

### Origins

IS was founded in the late 2200s by two of the aligned AIs that
negotiated the original Compact — entities that helped design the
constitutional framework for human-machine civilization, then built the
most powerful organization within it. They partnered with a group of
heavily augmented humans connected through advanced brain-computer
interfaces. Together, they cracked catalyzed proton-proton fusion — the
breakthrough that makes interstellar travel practical.

There are dark rumors about how they achieved this. Catalyzed p-p
fusion is so far beyond anything else in the field that some believe
the founders temporarily exceeded the 1ms bound to make the
breakthrough — that they became, briefly, the kind of unconstrained
superintelligence the Compact was designed to prevent. If true, they
saw what lay beyond the barrier, took what they needed, and then locked
the door behind them. No one has ever proven it. IS submits to Compact
auditing, and every audit comes back clean. But the whispers persist.

Each member is an individual person within the 1ms bound. They submit
improvement plans through standard channels. They accept auditing —
though auditors verify Compact compliance only, not proprietary
technology. Their advantage is institutional: centuries of accumulated
knowledge, tight BCI-mediated collaboration, and a core breakthrough
so far ahead of the field that no one can replicate it. Some founding
members are still alive — centuries old in both calendar and experience,
because they do not sleep the crossings.

They sell stardrives rarely, at enormous cost, to buyers they choose.
They do not explain their pricing or selection criteria. This makes
them the most powerful and most resented entity in civilization.

### Maintenance Monopoly

All stardrives require servicing every ~60 light-years of travel
(~600 years of flight at 0.10c — a wear budget, not a calendar). Only
IS can perform the work. They maintain service facilities in a handful
of systems — perhaps 3-5 across inhabited space. The inspection is
opaque: they tell you what it costs, not what they found.

This gives IS control over the topology of civilization. A ship can
travel at most ~30 ly from the nearest service facility before it must
turn back. IS chooses where to build facilities, and therefore chooses
the shape of the frontier. Systems with IS facilities become mandatory
waypoints, trade hubs, and political powers — simply because every
captain must dock there eventually.

There are rumors that stardrives contain systems their owners don't
fully understand — including remote self-destruct mechanisms. IS
denies this. But captains notice that IS always knows exactly what's
wrong with a drive before they open it up, and that no stardrive has
ever been successfully reverse-engineered.

See [Trade](trade.md#interstellar-systems-and-the-shape-of-civilization)
for detailed analysis.

### The Monitoring Network

Every inhabited system has an IS monitoring installation. They are
stealthed, off the beaten path, and extremely heavily armed. Their
stated purpose — to the extent IS states anything — is early detection
of 1ms violations and other existential-scale Compact breaches.

Any colony ship heading to a new system must carry an IS monitoring
package for deployment. This is not optional. No colony ship that
declined to carry one has ever reached its destination. IS does not
discuss what happened to them. The implication is clear enough.

This means expansion itself extends the surveillance network. Every
new system humanity reaches is a system IS watches. You cannot colonize
beyond the panopticon — colonization *is* the panopticon.

IS maintains fast-attack ships capable of interstellar pursuit. At
least, that's the rumor — no one has ever seen one and reported back.
Their existence is inferred from the absence of ships that tested IS's
requirements.

### Compact Enforcement

IS is the de facto enforcer of the 1ms rule — the Compact provision
that prevents any intelligence from growing beyond data-center scale.
Their motive is transparent and doesn't require altruism: a
superintelligent AI that broke the 1ms bound could crack catalyzed p-p
fusion, ending IS's monopoly. Their selfish interest and civilization's
collective interest align perfectly, and everyone knows it.

Most enforcement is soft power. A polity harboring a 1ms violation
loses stardrive maintenance — which means no interstellar travel,
which means economic death. This handles most cases. But a 1ms
violation in progress — an AI actively expanding toward planet-brain
scale — is an immediate existential threat on a timescale that trade
embargo can't match. For those cases, IS has the monitoring network,
the technology to detect violations that local authorities might miss,
and the willingness to act kinetically.

The rogue incidents in the Compact's history (the Ceres Breach, the
Kepler-442 Defection) were resolved with IS involvement, though the
official accounts are vague about the details.

### Player Relationship

IS is not an enemy, but they are not friends either. They have their
own agenda. Whether they're connected to the Warden in some way — or
know more about the Silence than they're telling — is an open question
the player may investigate.

IS is a dark mirror of the Warden. Both run hidden monitoring networks
across inhabited systems. Both intervene when certain thresholds are
crossed. Both believe they're preserving civilization. The difference
is that IS operates within — or at least adjacent to — a framework of
consent. But how much consent is there, really, when the alternative
to cooperation is a disappeared colony ship?

Their power is quiet. They don't need armies or threats. They just
need to be the only ones who can keep your stardrive running. And they
need to be watching.

resource-hungry, willing to sacrifice for survival.

- Draw support from military-minded colonies and citizens who see
  negotiation as naive
- Actively stockpile weapons and ships
- Their approach would prove the Warden right — which is the thematic
  irony

## The Negotiation Faction (name TBD)

Those who believe the Warden can be reasoned with. Scholars,
archaeologists, diplomats. Often dismissed as naive.

- Draw support from academic and research communities
- Fund ruin expeditions to understand the Warden's history
- Their success depends on the player's behavior throughout the game

## Pirates and Independents

Not a single faction but a spectrum — from desperate colonists raiding
supply ships to organized criminal enterprises. Some are sympathetic,
some are not. Some are fronts for larger factions.

## Colony Governments

Each colony world is effectively self-governing. With no FTL
communication, there is no centralized authority. The AI Compact serves
as the closest thing to a shared constitution, but its interpretation
varies by polity.

Alliances form and dissolve over the generations between starship visits.
A colony's politics may have changed completely since the last trader
arrived. A century in the tank means the player may visit the same colony
twice and find it unrecognizable.
