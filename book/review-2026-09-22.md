# State Review — 2026-09-22

Scope: the physics in `book/ship-systems.md`, the ship sim
(`crates/hf-game/src/ship/`), the rest of the code, and
`modeling/ship.py`. Every number below was recomputed, not copied.
The reactor sim was ported line-for-line to Python to run it past
the 2 s the Rust tests cover. The model was rendered headless.

Severity: **critical** = breaks the premise or makes the game
unplayable; **significant** = a wrong number that other numbers rest
on; **minor** = stale or cosmetic.


## Summary

1. **The collector hides two gimmes.** Stopping the intake stream
   is a petawatt energy flow the bible never books. And a coil on a
   40 m bow cannot throw a 14,000 km field. The 0.12c "conservation
   wall" is an artifact of the first problem, not a law.
2. **Low-grade heat has nowhere to go.** A few GW of hotel load at
   electronics temperature needs more radiator than the whole hull.
3. **The reactor sim runs away ~8 s after launch at any setpoint,
   disrupts, and never restarts.** The tests are shaped to finish
   before that happens. The "cascade" test passes with every radiator
   zone open.
4. **The game does not build.** The local graphix checkout changed
   API under the same 0.9.0 version. HUD and helm read paths that no
   longer exist.
5. **Structure is sized for the wrong load in two places.** The drive
   mounts carry the pod's weight, not the thrust (9.4× low). The hull
   skin carries one bay's cargo, not the ship (4× low).
6. **The Whipple water budget is 40 kt; a 1 m barrel is ~190 kt.**

What holds up is listed at the end. It is most of the arithmetic.


## 1. Physics — `book/ship-systems.md`

### P1 · critical · The intake energy is unbooked, and the wall is not a law

Lines 249–258 and 1170–1177. In the ship frame every captured proton
arrives with (γ−1)mc² ≈ 4.7 MeV. To put it in a tank you must take
that energy out. A magnetic field does no work, so only an electric
field (a direct converter) or collisions (heat) can.

| | Bubble, 0.1 cm⁻³ | Dense cloud, 1 cm⁻³ |
|---|---|---|
| Intake at 0.10c | 3.1 kg/s | 31 kg/s |
| ½ṁv² to remove | **1.4 PW** | **14 PW** |
| vs. 37 GW radiator | ~40,000× | ~400,000× |

Even at 900 km/s in the Bubble the stopping power is ~38 GW, which is
the whole hotel radiator.

Dumping it as heat is impossible, so it has to be converted, and then
it is not waste. It is a ram-driven power plant roughly as large as
the torch. Feed it to the exhaust and v_ex = √(v_e² + v²). Thrust at
v = v_e is still 0.41·ṁv_e. The ideal ramjet has no wall at v_e. The
ṁ(v_e − v) law only holds if you throw the ram energy away, and you
can't.

**Correction (2026-10-10).** An earlier version of this section
proposed an electrostatic decelerator whose waste heat would set the
speed limit, "~40 GW at η ≈ 97%". That was off by 1000×: 3% of
1.4 PW is **42 TW**. Holding converter waste to 10 GW would take
η ≈ 99.9993%, and the converter cannot be compact anyway. See
[Collector sizing](collector-sizing.md). The way out is to never stop
the stream: burn it in passing.

### P2 · critical · The collector is a second black box

Lines 1288–1294: "Superconducting coils on the bow — yards build
them … throw a magnetic field ~14,000 km ahead."

- **Pressure balance.** The ISM ram pressure at 0.10c is ρv² ≈ 1.5×10⁻⁷ Pa
  in the Bubble. A field holds the plasma off only where
  B²/2μ₀ ≥ ρv², i.e. B ≳ 600 nT. A 43 m loop at 20 T falls as
  (R/r)³ and reaches 600 nT at **~11–14 km**, not 14,000 km. To stand
  off at 14,000 km the winding would need ~10¹⁰ T. The best
  superconductors manage ~40 T.
- **Collection ∝ r².** A 1,000× smaller radius means 10⁶× less
  intake, and the parachute loses the same factor. The whole cruise
  economy rests on the 14,000 km number.
- **Ionization.** A field only catches ions. The local clouds are
  mostly neutral hydrogen. Photo-ionizing the swept volume ahead
  costs orders of magnitude more power than the ship has.
- **Funnel vs. mirror.** A converging field reflects most incoming
  ions (the mirror effect). That is why real analyses find magnetic
  scoops act as brakes. "Parachute" is what the physics does by
  default; "funnel" is the part that needs the miracle.

This is the same problem the antimatter decision turned on: a gimme
that isn't admitted as one. Commit `3e7caba` made the collector
explicitly *not* IS and yard-buildable. The honest options:

- (a) IS sells the collector with the box. The field is part of the
  secret, and the politics get sharper: no box, no scoop.
- (b) A second vendor, a second black box, admitted as such.
- (c) A real-physics scoop (tens of km) and a tank-limited rocket.
  This kills the 30 ly civilization.

Your call. Only (a) and (b) keep the setting.

### P3 · critical · Low-grade heat

Line 813 ("Hotel, plugs, tin pumps, and compute are a few GW") and
`CRUISE_DEMAND_W = 6 GW` (`constants.rs:29`). Electronics, cryo warm
ends, the hab, and the FEL's RF and cryo systems reject heat at
300–500 K, not into 1300–1850 K tin. The bible's own heat-pump
argument (line 958) forbids lifting it. At εσT⁴:

| Load | at 400 K | at 600 K |
|---|---|---|
| 6 GW | 4.6 km² | 0.9 km² |
| 1 GW | 0.77 km² | 0.15 km² |
| Whole hull lateral area | 0.25 km² | |

Lines 669 and 718 book the reactor's generation losses on the tin
loop and let the beam leave. That part is fine. What isn't booked is
the loads' own losses. A GW-class FEL at 10–30% wall-plug efficiency
dumps most of its draw into its RF and cryo plant at low temperature.
The sim also never turns delivered electricity into heat anywhere
(`thermal.rs` only sees reactor waste).

Suggested shape:

- Cruise hotel in the tens of MW.
- A separate, honestly sized low-temperature radiator.
- Combat electrical heat goes into the LH₂ as a sink. Warming it
  20→300 K absorbs ~4 MJ/kg, and the torch doesn't care that its
  hydrogen is warm. That couples combat heat to reaction mass, which
  is good study-sim material.

### P4 · significant · The drive mounts carry the pod, not the thrust

Lines 113–118 and `ship.py:178`. `F = nacelle_mass_t × 9 g` gives
2.2 GN. That is the pod's own inertia. The A-frame pushes the *rest
of the ship*: (520 − 50)/2 kt × 9 g = **20.7 GN per nacelle**, 9.4×
more.

- The code also loads one strut pair with all of F (`P = F/√2`). With
  the bible's one pair in compression and one in tension, each strut
  carries F/(2√2). Net, the struts are sized ~4.7× light.
- **At 3.6 m OD** the Euler wall comes out ~1 m, a near-solid rod and
  ~10 kt of struts.
- **At OD 6–8 m** it is 0.1–0.2 m and 2–4 kt.
- The belt rings are fixed sections, not sized from load. They carry
  all 41 GN into the hull.
- Expect the mounts at ~7–10 kt, not ~6 kt.

The A-frame argument itself still stands. A radial boom would be
10× worse than the text says.

### P5 · significant · The skin is sized per bay, not for the ship

`ship.py:264–267` sizes the skin from `P_bay` (9.1 GN), one bay's
own cargo. Thrust enters at the torque box, aft of the aft hold. The
skin at the aft end of `cargo_aft` pushes everything forward of it:
~430 kt × 9 g ≈ **38 GN**. Buckling thickness goes √(P), from
0.139 m to **~0.28 m**.

- **As a bare shell.** Load ramps up from the nose to the torque box
  (~790 m), so t averages ~0.19 m and the skin is ~50 kt. That is more
  than the whole 28 kt "internal structure" line.
- **As a stiffened shell.** Counting the rails as stringers would cut
  that a lot.

Neither version is computed anywhere yet.

Line 23's "tens of thousands of G" averages the load over the whole
5,000 m² cross-section. The real structure is Euler- and
buckling-limited at 9 g, as the mount sizing itself shows.

### P6 · significant · Whipple water: 40 kt booked, ~190 kt needed

Line 768 books 40,000 t. Line 412 says ~1 m thick, and "dive the
hull" (line 431) needs a metre a person can enter.

- **Barrel.** Over the 770 m of non-radiator barrel at r = 40, 1 m of
  water is **191 kt**; 2 m is 377 kt.
- **What 40 kt buys.** 0.21 m spread over the barrel.
- **Nose.** The 105 m cone alone is 14,000 m², so 14 kt per metre of
  water.

This is the same bug class the r = 300 m finding was. Shrinking the
radius narrowed it but never closed it. Either the water costs
~150 kt of cargo, or the barrel is ~20 cm and diving is local (thick
patches over the hab and passenger tracks).

### P7 · significant · No lateral thrust, no attitude control, engine-out torque

- **No lateral thrust.** The boxes fire along ±Z only. There is no
  RCS anywhere in the bible, yet line 598 plans a 0.1 G lateral dodge
  (~0.5 GN sideways on 500 kt). Rotating a 1 km hull (I ≈ 4×10¹³
  kg·m²) on differential nacelle thrust takes ~10 s at full combat
  thrust and minutes at cruise thrust. It is a turn, not a
  translation.
- **One control axis.** Two thrust lines in one plane give yaw about
  one axis only. A CG offset out of that plane cannot be trimmed
  (line 209's lopsided load).
- **Engine-out.** One box at r = 91 m is a 2 TN·m torque at 9 g. Line
  644, "a nacelle hit takes one engine and one eye", actually means
  the ship cannot burn at all without thrust vectoring or RCS.

That is a Vmc-style engine-out mechanic waiting to be written down.

### P8 · significant · The dense-cloud table exceeds the nozzle rating

Lines 303–314 and 1192–1200 vs. line 1069 (3.2 PW continuous, 11 PW
hard). Fusing the intake at 0.12c:

| Speed | Intake (1 cm⁻³) | Torch power | vs. rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.06c | 19 kg/s | 11.7 PW | above the 11 PW hard limit |
| 0.10c | 31 kg/s | 19.5 PW | 6× the continuous rating |

Nozzle-limited, the torch can fuse ~5 kg/s. Peak accel in a dense
cloud is ~0.02–0.03 G, not 0.068 G. The Bubble rows (÷10) are fine.

### P9 · significant · Refill at cruise is ~6 years, not 1–2

Line 1259. Holding 0.10c with the scoop on means the torch must
cancel the ram drag ṁv. That takes v/v_e = 83% of the intake,
leaving 17% to bank. That is ~6 years to fill 100 kt in the Bubble.

Scooping while coasting costs 0.019 G of drag, which is ~5,900 km/s
per year. The conclusion (full tanks on arrival) can still hold
through braking, but only if the parachute *collects*. The bible
says it reflects (line 1212), and reflecting collects nothing.

### P10 · minor · Stale ³He numbers

Line 1270 says the reactor burns "~27 t/year" at full power. That is
the 500 GW plant. At 100 GW it is 5.4 t/yr (line 826; the sim gives
4.5 t/yr actual). So the Bubble skim of 0.5 t/yr breaks even at
~10% power, not "a few percent". Lines 828 and 1271–1273 need
re-deriving.

### P11 · minor · FEL energy is missing the spot-fill factor

Line 662. A 1 m director at ~1 µm makes a ~1 m spot at 1,000 km. A
100 µm grain intercepts ~10⁻⁸ of the beam and a 1 mm grain ~10⁻⁶.
Per grain that is **MJ, not mJ–kJ**, before the bow-plasma tax. The
"GW-class" conclusion survives; the "megawatts on one easy target"
line doesn't. Shorter wavelength (UV) buys it back as λ².

### P12 · minor · Local Bubble density

Line 288. ~0.1 cm⁻³ is the local *cloud* value (the LIC, G cloud).
The hot Bubble between the clouds is ~0.005 cm⁻³ and fully ionized.
That is a feature, not a bug: the map becomes cloud "air" separated
by near-vacuum, and route knowledge is where the air is. It does
change every "Bubble" number above by up to 20× between clouds.

### P13 · opportunity · Tritium is most of the neutrons

`reactor.rs:367` burns the D-D tritium in place (`f_T = 1`). At the
rated point that D-T burn is **8.5 of the 10.3 GW of neutrons**.

- Pump tritium out with the ash instead: neutrons drop to 1.75 GW.
- The first-wall load drops from 5.2 MW/m² (about 10× ITER) to under
  1 MW/m².
- The 0.6 t/yr of tritium decays (12.3 yr half-life) into 0.6 t/yr
  of ³He. That is a ³He source as large as the Bubble skim.

It gives the fuel cycle a real lever and a real hazard (tritium
storage).

### P14 · minor · The bible's power chain omits bremsstrahlung

Line 808: 70% of 90 GW. The code does 80.2% of (charged − brems),
with brems ≈ 11 GW, and gets the same 63 GW. Bremsstrahlung is
supposed to be the learnable envelope, so the bible should say
where it goes.

### C2 · critical · The reactor can't hold any setpoint

Ported `step`/`auto_control`/`thermal.step` and ran them.

| Command | T_i | β | Disruption | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boot at rated (the game default) | 80 → 330 keV | 1.14 | t ≈ 8 s | dead forever |
| Boot, command 6 GW cruise | same climb | same | t ≈ 8 s | column retracting the whole time |
| Shutdown | collapses to 0 in ~5 s | | | density stays 7.7×10²⁰ indefinitely |

- **After disruption.** `step` returns early on `Offline`
  (`reactor.rs:323`) and nothing restarts. `ColdShutdown`,
  `FieldCharging`, `Ignition`, `Throttling`, and `Starting` are never
  constructed. Pillar 4, the cold-start ritual, doesn't exist yet.
- **β trim.** It raises B, which raises τ_E ∝ B², which heats the
  plasma faster. The β controller feeds the runaway (the July finding,
  still true).
- **Shutdown.** The chamber keeps its fuel because fuel ions have no
  loss term (see C4). After a shutdown the tin freezes in ~40 min with
  all zones open. That is a good emergent failure, but there is no
  restart to experience it with.

The July memory called burn control "the binding next step". It is
still the binding next step.

### C3 · significant · The tests are shaped to pass

- `ignited_at_rated_operating_point` (`reactor.rs:781`) stops at 2 s,
  when P_f is 134 GW on its way to disruption, inside an 80–140 GW
  window.
- `thermal_cascade_on_overdraw` (`reactor.rs:799`) asserts
  `max_tin > 1550`, but tin starts at 1900 K, so that check is
  vacuous. It also passes with **all eight zones open**: the plasma
  disrupts on β at 3.1 s and drags headroom to 0.84. It cannot tell a
  thermal cascade from the plasma runaway.

These should fail today, and failing is useful. Hold rated for 60 s,
and run the cascade test with all zones open as a control.

### C4 · significant · Energy and particles aren't conserved

The doctrine asks for "failures as consequences of conserved physics".

- `reactor.rs:429`: converter input is `p_charged − p_brem`, the
  steady-state answer, not the actual transport outflow
  `(w_i + w_e)/τ_E`. In every transient the books don't balance.
- `reactor.rs:340–344`: the length throttle scales `w_i`/`w_e` and
  (implicitly) particle count with volume.
  - Lengthening creates hot plasma and fuel from nothing: ~5 GW and
    ~0.5 g/s at full slew, which is half the output at 10 GW.
  - Shortening deletes them instead of dumping them into the
    converters.
- `reactor.rs:489`: disruption deletes 90% of the stored energy
  (~50 GJ). It should land on the first wall as a thermal spike. That
  is the most dramatic event the sim has.
- `reactor.rs:382–383, 435`: aux heating (up to 5 GW) is free. It
  isn't charged to the bus.
- `reactor.rs:405–406`: fuel ions have no loss term, only burn. In a
  mirror, fuel streams out the ends at the same rate as ash, so real
  fueling is 20–50× the burn rate with recycling. That recycle loop
  is the "the loss cone always leaks" mechanic, and it is missing.
- Also missing: synchrotron loss (large at 9 T and 64 keV), the
  relativistic brems correction, and an ion-heating fraction that
  varies with T_e (`F_ION_HEATING` is fixed at 0.57).
