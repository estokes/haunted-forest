# Ruins and the Silence

The archaeological record of dead civilizations — the central mystery
of the setting.

## The Pattern

Ruins are common. Every star system humanity has explored shows signs
of past intelligence. The civilizations reached varying levels of
advancement — some barely spacefaring, some far beyond current human
capability — but all are gone. The consistency is chilling.

## What the Ruins Tell Us

- Life and intelligence are common in the galaxy
- Civilizations get a window of development (thousands to millions of
  years) before something ends them
- The endings are abrupt — not slow decline, but termination
- Some civilizations clearly fought back (and lost)
- Some appear to have tried diplomacy (and failed)
- Some individuals joined it. Their remains are richer — better
  tools, stranger wounds — and they ended the same way, a little
  later. The Warden uses local staff. It does not keep them.
- The Warden's intervention threshold is interstellar capability

## Key Ruin Sites (TBD)

Each ruin civilization needs:
- A species (biology, culture, what made them distinctive)
- How far they got before the Warden intervened
- What artifacts they left behind
- What their ruins reveal about the Warden
- How they tried to respond (fight, negotiate, hide, other?)

The player pieces together the Warden's history from multiple ruin
sites. Each site is a partial answer. Together they are how you learn
what a treaty can actually buy, and what joining costs after the work
is done. The discarded agents are the tell. A player who misses them
can still take the upgrades.

## Open Questions

- How many ruin civilizations do we need? (Probably 5-8 major ones)
- Which ones tried to fight? Which tried to negotiate?
- Did any come close to succeeding? What can the player learn from
  their near-misses?
- Are any ruins still dangerous (active defenses, environmental hazards)?
- Do any ruins contain Warden technology that can be studied?
